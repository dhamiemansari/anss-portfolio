SUKHA

Patient app · iOS · Android · 3 web portals

nine pillars, one deep breath

● OVERVIEW

A preventive-health clinic in India treats the whole person — brain, gut, sleep, heart and hormones. Its app had to turn that into a first session anyone could finish. I designed version one of Sukha — the app and three web portals — where every question explains itself and every answer leads somewhere.

● THE PROBLEM

Nine body systems.
One gentle first session.

The clinic’s method looks at brain, gut, sleep, heart, hormones and more. A first-time patient doesn’t want a clinical intake — they want to know where they stand and what to do next, without wading through a medical form.

01

Getting in

Phone, Google or Apple with a four-digit OTP — and a guest path for the curious.

02

A body baseline

Gender, age, weight and height — turned into a BMI patients can actually read.

03

The Life Index

A reflective survey across nine pillars, from cognitive vitality to mental health.

04

The care team

Admin, service-provider and concierge portals behind every patient journey.

THE BRIEF

“An app that feels like a deep breath, not a clinic form — and ends with a score the patient understands.”

So every first session had to answer —

01Who is this person?

02Where does their body stand?

03What should they do next?

● 01 — GETTING IN

Calm, from the
very first screen.

Sign-in sits under a hand-painted landscape instead of a wall of fields. Phone number first, Google and Apple one tap away, a guest path for browsers — and every OTP state designed: waiting, resending, wrong code, success.

01Choose a door

Google, Apple, a new account — or look around as a guest.

02Phone first

Country code and number up front; social sign-in one tap away.

03Four digits

A countdown before resend, and the number stays editable.

04Wrong code, kindly

The error sits under the boxes in clay — never a pop-up.

05You’re in

A painted moment of success before the profile questions.

● 02 — ABOUT YOU

Four questions,
one per screen.

Basic details are collected one at a time, with consent, each carrying a short line on why it’s asked. The answers come straight back as a BMI the patient can read.

  1. 1Why we askEvery personal question explains itself before the patient answers.
  2. 2Age, worked out for youA date wheel shows the exact age — years and days — as it turns.
  3. 3Units they think inWeight in kg, height in cm or feet, set on rulers instead of keyboards.
  4. 4A result, not a numberBMI on a coloured scale, with a plain-language note on what it means.

● 03 — LIFE INDEX

Nine pillars, one breath at a time.

The Sukha Life Index asks reflective questions across nine body systems. Between pillars, a short breathing pause resets attention — so a long assessment never feels like one.

01Consent first

What the index is, and a data-use agreement before any question.

02One question at a time

Five honest answers, from Never to Always.

03A breath between pillars

Breathe in, meet the next pillar, continue.

04Building your plan

Responses analysed, profile decoded, protocol designed.

Consent before questionsTerms and data use are agreed on the intro card, before the first question appears.
Progress you can seeA pillar counter and progress bar on every question — 1 of 9, 2 of 9.
Answers, not scalesNever, Rarely, Sometimes, Often, Always — nothing clinical to interpret.

● 04 — RESULTS

A score first,
then the story.

The summary opens on one composite index out of 100 and a colour band, then sets chronological age against physiological age. Below, a radar maps all nine pillars and bars break each one down.

WHILE YOU WAIT

Building the plan,
out loud.

Instead of a spinner, the app narrates its work in three plain steps — and the ring fills as each one completes.

● 06 — DESIGN LANGUAGE

● 07 — THE WHOLE PRODUCT

Every screen.

● 08 — WEB PORTALS

Same calm, three back offices.

Admin, service providers and the concierge team each get a portal. They share one split sign-in — painting left, form right — with forgot-password and OTP verification. Admin adds user management, filtered by role, specialisation and status.
